# Prose rules for this project

Every line of reader-facing copy in this piece gets checked against the list below.
Source: [tropes.fyi](https://tropes.fyi) by [ossama.is](https://ossama.is), supplied by
the author as a hard requirement.

Any one of these used once may be fine. Several together, or one used repeatedly, is the
failure mode.

## Sentence structure

- **Negative parallelism.** No "it's not X, it's Y." No "the question isn't whether, it's
  when." This is the single loudest tell.
- **"Not X. Not Y. Just Z."** Same thing, tripled.
- **False ranges.** "From X to Y" only when X and Y sit on a real scale with a meaningful
  middle.
- **Anaphora abuse.** Do not open three consecutive sentences the same way.
- **"The X? A Y."** No self-posed rhetorical questions answered in the next clause.
- **Comma-clipped trailing phrase.** Do not hang a bare noun or clipped clause off a comma
  to close a sentence.
- **"It's worth noting."** Also "importantly", "notably", "interestingly". Cut them.

## Paragraph and composition

- **Short punchy fragments.** No one-sentence paragraphs for manufactured emphasis.
- **Preamble.** Do not announce what the section is about to do. Deliver the point.
- **Premise stacking.** Do not lay three paragraphs of evidence before finally asking the
  question they imply.
- **Reasoning leak.** No narrating the writing's own decisions or the author's role.
- **Excessive enumeration.** No "The first... The second... The third..." prose listicles.
- **Fractal summaries.** No summary per subsection, per section, and per document.
- **One-point dilution.** Say it once, well.
- **Belaboring the unnecessary.** Do not defend an uncontroversial point against an
  objection nobody raised.
- **Signposted conclusion.** No "in conclusion", "to sum up", "in summary".
- **The tie-back.** Once the point lands, stop. Do not loop back to restate the question.
- **Never-ending conclusion.** Land one point and end.
- **"Despite its challenges."** Do not raise a problem only to wave it away.

## Tone

- **Grandiose stakes inflation.** Rarefaction curves are useful, not civilisational.
- **Compulsive counting.** Do not state the number of items before listing them.
- **Invented concept labels.** No coined "X paradox", "Y trap", "Z creep" presented as
  established terms.
- **Quotable one-liners.** No slide-bait that carries no information.
- **Forced figurative language.** The pizza analogy is the piece's one governing metaphor.
  Do not stack additional coined similes on top of it.
- **Promotional language.** Describe, do not sell.
- **Vague attributions.** Name Hurlbert 1971, Kruskal 1964, `vegan`, `scikit-bio`. Never
  "experts suggest" or "studies show".
- **Appeal to familiarity.** No "famously", "notoriously", "as we all know", "a classic".
- **"Think of it as..."** and **"Let's break this down."** No teacher-mode hand-holding;
  the reader is a graduate student.
- **False vulnerability.** No performative self-awareness.
- **Collaborative "we"** where the author means "you" or a plain statement.

## Word choice

- **Em dashes.** A couple in the whole piece, at most. Use commas, colons, full stops.
- **Magic adverbs.** "Quietly", "deeply", "fundamentally", "remarkably", "arguably".
- **Synonym cycling.** A pizzeria stays a pizzeria. It does not become an establishment,
  then a venue, then an eatery. Same for sample, count table, distance matrix.
- **Self-echo.** Do not resurface an earlier phrase as if paying it off.
- **"Tapestry", "landscape", "paradigm", "ecosystem", "load-bearing", "gated".**
- **"Delve", "leverage" (verb), "utilize", "robust", "streamline", "harness".**
- **The "serves as" dodge.** Use "is".
- **Superficial "-ing" tails.** No "highlighting its importance", "reflecting broader
  trends", "underscoring its role".

## Formatting

- **Title case headings.** Sentence case only.
- **Wh-word headings.** No "What this means", "Why it matters", "Where the data lives".
- **Bold-first bullets.** Avoid opening every list item with a bolded keyword.
- **Unicode decoration.** Straight quotes. Write `->` rather than an arrow glyph in body
  copy. (Real math symbols in formulas are fine.)
